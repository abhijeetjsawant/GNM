## 2. The defect, and why it is two defects

On the falling performer the captured hip line is off his own median (**215.0 mm** raw /
214.4 smoothed) by more than 15 % on **30 raw and 23 smoothed frames**. Classified per
camera they are two different failures, and the classification is what makes the row
defensible rather than a guess.

### 2.1 Class (i) — an inward collapse in agreeing views

Frames **110–119** (and **84–86**). The raw hip line reads **125–174 mm on nine of the ten**;
the tenth, frame 113, is an outward spike at **355 mm** that the smoothing turns into 160,
and the symmetric rule fires on it too.

| frames 109–119, both hips | A001 | B001 | C001 | D001 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| frames supporting both hips, of 11 | **11** | 1 | **11** | **9** |
| confidence on D001 | — | — | — | **0.43–0.93** |
| reprojection residual of the raw point, on the eight frames A, C and D support **alone** | \<7.4 px | — | \<5.2 px | \<8.1 px |
| worst pairwise ray angle | | | | **157.2–160.7°** |

Three supporting views, so D8's conditioning gate is silent by construction, and it is
*right* to be: the depth is determined. The point it is determined at is simply wrong,
because the 2D detector places both hips inward in **every** view.

**And the neighbours prove which way they moved.** Over 110–119 the root landmark holds
(root→neck 509–544 mm against a 515 median), both thighs hold (391.9–421.8 against 401.8 /
406.2) and **|hip_mid − root| holds (68.0–80.7 against 76.5)** — worst 5.5 % off, while the
hip line loses 42 %. The pelvis landmark sits 76.5 mm *off* the hip line, so a collapse
toward it would have moved that distance. It did not. The hips travel **along their own
line toward its midpoint**, and the knees do not follow.

**The same measurement separates the two sub-runs.** On 84–86 `|hip_mid − root|` reaches
**94.6 mm (+23.6 %)**, because there only **one hip moves per frame**: frame 84 reads 84 mm
from the root against a 127 mm right, frame 85 reads 138 against 103. A one-sided collapse
necessarily drags the midpoint. That is the card's "ONE HIP AT A TIME" read off a length
rather than asserted, and it is why 84–86 is the registered over-charge (§3.2).

### 2.2 Class (ii) — an outward stretch along the A–C baseline

Frames **158–168**. Only A001 and C001 see him; **D001 has no detection at all** and B001 is
sub-floor. Their worst pairwise ray angle is **139.96–140.70°** — *under* D8's 150° ceiling,
so the conditioning gate is correctly silent — and sin 140° ≈ 0.64 is quite enough to make a
*stable* triangulation of a *wrong* depth. Residuals 0.08–3.78 px.

He is lying on the floor (hip midpoint **153–166 mm** up) with the hip line **15.8–28.3°**
off both viewing rays. Splitting the hip vector into the A–C baseline direction and the
plane across it says what happened:

| | along the baseline | across it |
|---|---|---|
| frames 158–168 | **231.4 – 275.1 mm**, every frame | 17.7 – 74.0 mm |
| the honest frames (median) | 181.1 mm | 117.0 mm |

The whole run is stretched along the one axis a two-view pair cannot fix. **Six of the
eleven frames cross the ceiling** (158, 159, 163, 165, 167, 168 at 250–278 mm, +16–29 %) and
**the other five sit at +9–13 % under it and stay as they are**. A ceiling is a ceiling;
stated, not hidden.

### 2.3 The margin the row ships on

With the off frames removed the raw hip line spreads **−8.2 / +8.5 %** (performer 1) and
**−9.2 / +6.9 %** (performer 0) at p5–p95, inside the 15 % ceiling on both — and wider than
the legs' −5.1 / +6.2 %. That extra width is the exposure this row adds and it is stated
before any band, not after.

## 3. What ships, and the one convention that costs something

### 3.1 The row


### 3.2 Why both endpoints, and what it costs

The hips have a parent, so a per-hip `root→hip` rule was the *more precise* candidate. It was
measured, not dismissed. On the **same honest mask** the hip line's own margin is quoted on,

**The cost is registered in three places rather than described in one:** the `src/` comment,

### 5.3 Why demote wins here, where D8b feared it would not

D8b's own comment names demote's risk: *the rays are consistently wrong in every view, so the
reprojection term may out-vote the length term and put the point back where it was*. On the
hips it does not, and the reason is worth stating because it is the answer to the card's
"case for waiting". Withholding **both** endpoints of a segment whose only remaining
constraints are `RIGID_LIMBS`' own `root→hip` and `left_hip→right_hip` leaves the sequence
solve a slot it can only satisfy by restoring the performer's own width; a collapsed point
reprojects onto its own detections but cannot simultaneously satisfy the width, and the
recovery refuses it. The result is 3.9 mm against a 51.0 mm fault, and on the real take it is
23 → 0 frames off (§6.4) with the photographs agreeing (§6.1).

## 9. Findings beyond the bands

1. **A symmetric collapse is invisible to the delivered root, and a one-sided one is not.**
   Measured on the synthetic (1.62 → 1.65 mm against 15.04 mm) and confirmed on the take
   (R1's 3.15 mm median against R3's 2.09° of pelvis rotation). Anything that judges a hip
   repair by watching the root will see nothing on exactly the frames where the hips are most
   wrong.
2. **The delivered file is whole-take-coupled through its own sizing.** D3's per-performer
   rest is measured over the whole take, so changing eighteen frames changes sixteen rest
   bones and every frame's forward kinematics, and the foot contacts with them. Any
   "displacement should be local" prediction about a *delivered* quantity is wrong by
   construction in this pipeline; the same prediction about the *capture* stage is exactly
   right. Worth carrying into every later step's R-clauses.
3. **MAMMA cannot referee a hip width.** Its hip line is a per-subject constant about 97 mm
   narrower than ours, so a collapsed build scores *better* against it. This is the sharpest
   instance yet of "numbers from different references never share an axis", and it is the
   second time in this lane that a reference has rewarded a defect.
4. **The photographs and the joints disagree on class (ii), and both are right.** B1 sees
   nothing (−0.0008, spanning zero) because the error is along the two cameras' own baseline;
   B4 sees a complete repair (six frames off → zero). Neither is wrong: the silhouette scores
   the mesh's outline and the error is a depth error. A depth stretch is the failure mode a
   photograph instrument is structurally blind to, and this step is the demonstration.
5. **The frozen-arm control must be read per group.** Pooled travel on this fixture is
   494 mm, but that is the ankles of a squat clip; the hips travel 13 mm. A control quoted on
   the pooled figure would have looked strong and been nearly powerless on the cells the
   selector actually reads. D8b's fixture-v2 repair fixed the *clip*; this is the reminder
   that the same clip is fast for one landmark group and static for another.
6. **The 84–86 mode is the weakest arm in every instrument that can see it** — the synthetic
   (20.5 → 17.0 mm, against 50.3 → 3.9 for the symmetric mode), the converter (15.04 mm of
   root movement), and the over-charge itself. Three frames on this take; a take with more
   one-sided occlusion would be a different proposition.

## 10. The rule's own boundaries, and what every instrument is blind to

**What the row can and cannot do.**

* It is **self-referential**: the reference is the performer's own take median, so a
  take-long error is invisible and a limb welded to its own median passes perfectly. The
  fault must be a minority of the take. Asserted as a test.
* **A length invariant cannot score direction.** Rotating both hips 40° about their own
  midpoint changes nothing this rule can see. Nothing about D7's pelvis frame may be inferred
  from it; R3 measures that separately and only as a delta between two of our own answers.
  Asserted as a test.
* It **cannot say which endpoint is wrong**, and on the hips it does not try: it charges
  both, and over-charges the one-hip frames. §3.2, and a test.
* It **only sees what D8's gates leave it**. 25 of 150 frames were unmeasurable on performer
  1; frame 113's outward spike is among the twelve raw-off frames it never saw. §6.3.
* On class (ii) it fires on **six of eleven** frames and the other five stay as they are,
  under the ceiling at +9–13 %. That is not a partial success to be rounded up: it is what a
  ceiling does.

**What each instrument is blind to.**

| instrument | blind to |
|---|---|
| `captured_limb_stability.py` | truth; direction; a common-mode detector error (the residuals stay small and the angles stay wide — which is the whole defect); the rig and the mesh. |
| the synthetic | the real detector's bias (the injection is a fixed scaling); speed (the clips move slower than the take, and the *hips* barely move at all); rigidity (its bones are exactly rigid); **and class (ii) — no fixture in this lane realises a two-view depth stretch, and the 1.25 stretch arm is a 2D-consistent widening, a different fault with the same sign.** |
| B1, the photographs | depth; a fore-aft mirror; where inside its own outline a joint sits. On a body lying down under two cameras whose baseline is the error's axis, a depth correction is close to invisible **by construction**. It scores the MESH, on an asset whose proportions are not the performer's. |
| B3, `delivered_vs_capture --reference raw` | it is **biased against the repair on exactly the fired frames**, because there the reference IS the point the rule judged wrong. Read the legs, the feet and the trunk floor. |
| B4 | the candidate optimises it directly. In the merge rule only PAIRED with B1, and read on a fixed denominator so a slid reference cannot pass as a repair. |
| B5, MAMMA | it has no truth either (`gt_*` are byte-copies of `pred_*`), and on this quantity its joints are not our joints. §6.5. |
| B6, the D3 gate | the length rule **never runs there**. It is a src-hygiene tripwire and nothing more. |
| R1–R4 | truth; direction at the pelvis; the mesh. They say what moved. |
| the head gate | a branch build — it reads the shipped head solve from disk. |

## 11. What is open after this step

* **The one-hip over-charge.** Registered in `SEGMENT_LENGTH_RULES`' provenance entry, in the
  `src/` comment and as a test. A per-hip rule needs a tighter pelvis landmark than this
  detector gives; if the pelvis frame gets its own repair (D7c), the honest `root→hip` spread
  is worth re-measuring before this convention is called permanent.
* **Class (ii) is confirmed by no fixture.** Whether `demote` genuinely recovers a two-view
  depth stretch, rather than merely withholding it and letting the solve interpolate, is
  answered here only by B4 (which the candidate optimises) and by B1 (which cannot see it).
  A fixture that injects a *depth* error on a two-camera pair — moving the 3D point along the
  pair's baseline and re-projecting, rather than moving the 2D detections — would settle it,
  and does not exist in this lane.
* **The ceiling's own remedy stays open.** 0.15 is still a seed from one take's legs, and the
  hip line's honest spread (−9.2 / +8.5 %) is markedly wider than the legs' (−5.1 / +6.2 %),
  so the margin this row ships on is thinner than the one D8b shipped on. A worse take makes
  this rule withhold more.
* **Two `src/` comments are now inaccurate and were deliberately left.** The tuple's header
  says the shoulder line is the only segment without a parent whose endpoints are both
  charged; `_reject_inconsistent_segments`' `segment_length_note` says the same. The brief
  for this step permitted one row and nothing else. They should be corrected in the next
  touch of that file.
* **R2's finding is owed forward.** Every later step's "displacement should be local"
  prediction needs the capture/delivery split this step had to discover, or it will fail for
  the pipeline's reasons and be read as the step's.
* **Three pre-existing test failures** (`test_body_compositor`, `test_body_export`,
  `test_phase4_app`) reproduce exactly with `src/` reverted and are unrelated to this lane
