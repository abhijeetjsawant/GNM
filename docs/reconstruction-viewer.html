What you are looking at

Every joint here was triangulated from four views by our own geometry. Nothing from the reference system is in the runtime — it supplied the footage and the calibration for this fixture, and nothing else. The two performers were separated by our own association stage, which we measured this week at zero identity switches across the take even while their surfaces come within 2 mm.

Turn on the delivered rig and watch the limbs. The grey figure is what actually leaves the pipeline: a generic skeleton with fixed bone lengths, driven by the reconstructed joints. It is a different body from the one we measured — arms 16–29 mm too long, legs 15–30 mm too short, and hips 180 mm apart against a measured 207. That mismatch is 75–79 mm root-relative, and no detector improvement reaches it.
What this is not. It is not an accuracy claim. The footage and calibration come from a research fixture we may not ship, we own no multi-camera footage of our own, and no number in this project has ever been checked against physical ground truth. What you are seeing is that the system runs end to end and produces a coherent two-person track — not that it is correct.
Reconstructed positions are the pipeline's own triangulated_world_positions_z_up_m; the rig is forward-kinematic from the delivered local_rotations_xyzw. Both are read straight from the run's retained artifacts, not re-simulated for this page.
