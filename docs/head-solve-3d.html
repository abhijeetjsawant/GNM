Head Solve in Three Dimensions

The head we shipped could not move relative to the chest. This is what replaced it — drawn in the world it was solved in, where no camera ray can hide an error.

The solve, in 3D

Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, scrub or play the take. The yellow axis runs up through the skull, blue to the performer's left, red forward. The dashed grey gizmo is the head we used to deliver — carried by the torso, holding this take's average head pose. It is not still: it turns whenever the torso turns, median 2.4–2.8° a frame. What it cannot do is turn relative to the chest, and that is the whole defect.

Watch the dashed gizmo: it turns only when the torso turns. That is the entire defect — one line of the retarget gave the head, the neck and both eyes the torso's frame, so head orientation relative to the chest was 0.000000° on every frame of every capture.

The same instant, from all four cameras

The overlay is the weaker evidence, and deliberately shown second. A projected overlay proves placement and never accuracy: error along a camera's own ray is invisible in it. The median separation between the two gizmos across these four views is only 3–5 px at source scale — where it looks small, projection is hiding the angle, not the angle being small.

Frames are evenly spaced and were chosen before any angle was computed; an earlier version of this figure greedy-picked the frames that maximised the very angle it displayed. The gizmo is drawn at the fitted Head landmark inside the skull, not at the solve's template origin, which sits near the collar.

Performer 1 misses by 0.28° and the page says so. An earlier report of this work claimed a pass on both. That measurement was taken on the prototype's rotations, not the pipeline's — and the two chose different smoothing weights, so it was two true statements about two different heads. This table is the configuration a capture actually delivers.

The cause was mine: porting the solver into the library, I replaced “average each camera's median residual” with “one median over all observations”. A camera that sees more of the head contributes more observations, so pooling is weighted by coverage and lets the best-placed view decide — a same-denominator violation. Restored, both performers now select the same weight, and performer 0 moved from fail to pass. Performer 1 remains 0.28° outside.

Drawn from the pipeline's own solve, in capture world, metres, Z-up. The gate measures parity with an instrument on a tracking metric — never accuracy, which nothing in this lane can yet claim.
