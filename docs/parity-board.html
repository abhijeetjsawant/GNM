01

The honest position

No number in this project has ever been checked against ground truth.

Every millimetre figure is measured against MAMMA's own output — an estimate, not truth — or against synthetic data that contains none of calibration error, lens distortion, sync error, soft-tissue artefact or joint-definition error. All five are first-order on real footage.

The marker session is the only path to an accuracy claim, and it is not booked. Until it lands, every band below is a consistency band, not an accuracy band.

The target is not 13.5 mm, and this board said so until 2026-08-30. That figure is MPJPE against a fitted reference — Vicon markers passed through a model fit — and it is read off a chart, not a published table. On held-out markers, the measurement behind the parity claim, the reference system scores 22.48 mm against a marker pipeline's own 21.62 mm. State three numbers, never one: MPJPE 12.96 · PVE 17.18 · held-out marker 22.48.

And this is why the session gate matters more than the number. Two marker-grade instruments sit 0.86 mm apart at ~22 mm, so marker ground truth cannot discriminate below about 20 mm. The session can tell us whether we are at 25 or 45. It can never tell us whether we are at 13 or 18 — a sub-20 mm claim needs a different instrument entirely, a body scan or synthetic truth.

01
Measured · known problem

2D detection — SOMA-77 / Apple Vision / MediaPipe

The gap, and it moved today. Not the tail — the bulk and the coherent bias. Our p50 is 9.33 px against MAMMA's 4.29 (~2.2×), plus roughly 18.6 px of convention offset that passes through triangulation untouched.

And it discards 60 of 77 joints — but it does not delete them. The adapter maps 17 of SOMA-77's 77, dropping every finger chain, both toe joints, HeadEnd, Jaw and the clavicles. All 77 are nevertheless retained on disk, every person, every frame, all four cameras — proven by 20,043 identity checks with zero mismatches. This board previously said measuring them "needs a worker re-run, not a re-read". That was wrong. Toes, HeadEnd, Jaw and 30 finger joints are measurable now. nose is index 6, the Head skeletal joint, and sits behind the eyes.

The first of the dropped joints has now been measured, and it failed. HeadEnd — the head lead the plan called most promising — triangulates a rigid skull segment at 194–254 mm against an anatomical 110–140, with a standard deviation exceeding the anatomical quantity itself. That is the phalanx pattern one length scale up. Head landmarks are SOMA-77's weakest region, and the separation is in the tail: cross-view pair spread p95 reaches 1,805 mm on the eyes against 76–141 mm on every limb control. Scored on one frame set against the other two integrated adapters, Apple Vision's ear axis is the only head axis that stays inside its own detector's body-control range — and on segments of nearly equal length it is 5.5–6.4× tighter than SOMA-77's skull axis. It survives a null template that predicts the ears from nose, eyes and neck with no ear evidence: the null is 7–20× worse. This is regional, not a detector swap — SOMA-77's body is better, and Apple Vision's ears resolve on only two thirds of frames at 2.4–2.7 cameras.

But the head's 2D are the best thing this detector produces. Symmetric epipolar distance per camera pair — the one test that is blind to depth by construction — puts every head landmark on both detectors at 0.29–0.78× its own detector's body control, never above 1.0. Four cameras agree on a single 3D head point better than they agree on a knee. So the head's failure is not detection; it is entirely depth — rays to a 120–160 mm feature at 5 m are near-parallel relative to it, so excellent 2D still triangulates into decimetres of scatter along the viewing direction. That is an estimator problem, and it is the same finding that sent the fingers to a model-constrained fit — which passed this test at 1.2×, less cleanly than the head does.

The ear lead was measured twice more and is now closed. Scored on direction rather than length, the ear axis scatters at 21–41° of yaw, larger than the whole head-on-torso signal — because a differential depth error is first order in an axis's direction and second order in its length, so a length invariant cannot score direction. Put into a rigid-head fit's objective instead, the ears made it worse, carrying 1.3–1.6× the reprojection residual of SOMA-77's head joints despite being the most cross-view-consistent landmarks measured. An ear is a surface landmark that slides over the skull as the head turns — every camera sees the same apparent point, and it is not a fixed place on the bone. That is this lane's founding detector argument arriving at the head, and it says the head needs landmarks that stay on the bone, not a wider baseline.

And then the ear axis lost its recommendation to a second measurement, which is the more useful half. All of the above scores a segment's length. Scored on direction against MAMMA, the same ear axis scatters at 21–41° of yaw — larger than the entire head-on-torso signal it would explain. The mechanism generalises: for an axis roughly perpendicular to the camera's depth direction, a differential depth error between its endpoints is first order in direction and second order in length, so a length invariant cannot score direction — the companion to this lane's standing "reprojection cannot score depth". The ears belong in the objective of a multi-frame fit, not on a direct path to a rotation channel.

Blind to — no ground truth. Scored against MAMMA's own fit, which is deflated because its fit consumed the landmarks it is scored against. The head comparison is stability, which is convention-free; it says nothing about which landmark is anatomically closer to truth, and an ear, an eye and a joint centre are different points under different definitions.

Touches every joint and moves already-triangulated ones by 5.6–7.9 mm median, with a tail past 0.9 m. Those large moves are outlier repair, not damage — joints it moves furthest had 34–46× the raw temporal acceleration. It is the pipeline's de facto outlier filter.

06
Measured · known problem

Positions → rotations, and ground projection

The largest displacement between what we reconstruct and what we deliver: 75–79 mm root-relative median. Analytic and closed-form — there is no IK solver. Of that, 36–47 mm on the arms is a converter defect: the clavicle direction is measured from a synthetic torso anchor but applied about the rig's own shoulder origin, 15–19° off. Fixable now.

And this stage used to emit a head that was a constant — one line assigned Spine, Chest, UpperChest, Neck, Head and both eyes the torso frame verbatim, so every shipped track carried the identity quaternion on those joints and head-orientation-relative-to-thorax spread was 0.000000°. Fixed in the pipeline, not yet in the artifact on disk. The head is now solved by a pipeline stage, and a rebuild produces 26.5° and 20.7° of median local head rotation. The delivered body-track.npz files predate this and still carry the identity quaternion — they were written before the stage existed and have not been regenerated. Until they are, what a user receives is still the constant. The eyes follow the head; the neck still keeps the torso frame, which is deliberate and unmeasured rather than solved. Both feet are still welded to the torso frame too — a separate loop two dozen lines further down, not the same lines, and unchanged by this session. "Welded" rather than "frozen": the torso frame is rebuilt every frame, so a welded joint turns when the torso turns and cannot turn otherwise.

Why this is on the board and not only in a head document. Scored on composed world head rotation — the statistic a reasonable person reaches for first — the constant reads 6.64° p95 on one performer against MAMMA's 6.62°. Parity to 0.3%, while carrying no head information at all. On the other performer the same constant reads 2.0× MAMMA — so the naive gate's verdict depends on which performer you scored, which is worse than failing cleanly. "No gate a constant can pass" is normally a warning about a gate someone might write; here it describes what ships. The head gate's negative control is therefore not a construction — it is the delivered artifact.

Blind to — this is delivered-vs-captured, a different reference and population from the detector numbers. Do not subtract them. And the head figures are tracking statistics of an estimate on both sides: MAMMA is an instrument, never truth, so head parity is measurable here and head accuracy is not.

Built · prototype · off the delivery path

Parametric body fit — MHR + momentum

This stage now exists. Meta's momentum (MIT) calibrates an MHR body per performer against our own four-camera landmarks and solves pose per frame. Mean segment error 20.4 mm against 26.1 mm for MHR's untuned mean body — it beats doing nothing, which the first attempt did not. Both performers render onto the plate at fitted-character.html.

What it does not fix. Only the skeleton is fitted. Girth and mass stay at MHR's mean body, because 19 joint centres carry no surface information — perturbing all 45 identity parameters moves the joints by 0.000000 mm. Dense shape still waits on the body scan.

A hard stop, and it is new. Bone length and locator offset are not separately identifiable from sparse landmarks. Free, the solver puts a median 84 mm between landmark and joint — not anatomically plausible. Pinned at zero, it asserts the landmark is the joint, false by up to 33.9 mm. This is an independent argument that the marker session unblocks a build, not merely a validation.

Blind to — the overlay proves placement, never accuracy: error along the camera's own ray is invisible in projection. The silhouette check is coarse — at 5.9 mm/px its scatter is 61 mm, good only for catching a gross fault. Placement rests on the 3D figure, 32.9 mm root-to-root. Still off the delivery path; the export schema gap below is unchanged.

Head, feet and hands do not work, and not for solver reasons. The rig has a full head chain, ball-of-foot joints and forty finger joints; the landmarks drive none of them. On this arm the head jitters at p95 44° per frame against 8° for the knee on the same solve — the knee is bracketed by landmarks and the head rests on a 59 mm eye baseline whose standard deviation is 133 mm. The foot has no rotation channel at all.

Two heads, two different defects — do not quote them as one pipeline. The 44° above is this prototype's local head rotation. The delivered path's head is the opposite failure: frozen (stage 06). A fix for one is not a fix for the other, and a session that measures only one will conclude the wrong thing. Both, plus the measured MAMMA bar and a pre-registered gate with two failing controls, are in HEAD_ORIENTATION_MEASURED.md; the diagnosis is HEAD_FEET_HANDS_PLAN.md. A visual report of what the solve produces — the gate, the traces, and the four-camera overlays — is at Head Orientation Solve.

08
Not owned

Calibration, sync, lens distortion

Every result runs on MAMMA's camera rig, which is MPI-derived and barred from shipping. We have never measured a sync residual on anything of ours. Half a frame at 30 Hz costs 29–48 mm at our joint speeds — and MAMMA's own genlocked rig still slipped two frames.

A sixth absent term, and it cuts both ways. Rolling shutter is tracked nowhere — per-row readout means genlocked cameras still sample head and feet at different instants, and measured readouts run 8.7–26.8 ms against a 5 ms budget. But that same row structure is the cheapest sub-millisecond sync instrument there is: a flash lights only a band of scanlines, and the band's row index encodes sub-frame phase to 0.3–0.5 ms under an MIT licence. Global shutter removes the error term and the instrument.

The dominant answer is neither. A GPIO hardware trigger holds under 100 ns — three to four orders of magnitude better than any software method. If the cameras accept one, nothing else here matters.

Consequence — no shipped constant may be fitted on a MAMMA-derived artifact. An owned multicam fixture can precede the marker session, would retire this, and is now load-bearing for three separate questions: sync, the sequence solve, and Step 4's training data.

What runs today

End‑to‑end The pipeline produces a character

Frames → detection → association → triangulation → solve → smoothing → 55-joint rig → GLB. It runs. What is missing is any defensible claim about how good it is.

89.1% Joints triangulated directly

Against a structural ceiling of 89.5% — we are at 99.6% of what this detector can reach. The remaining 10% is two ear joints SOMA-77 never emits, not a quality signal.

Clean Licence position

No MAMMA code, weights or outputs in the runtime. MHR is dual-provenance and can be re-homed if the NVIDIA terms ever become a problem.

13–16° The head bar, measured not assumed

MAMMA's head moves this much relative to the thorax on this footage, median, with about 29° of range. That is the signal a head solve has to explain, and our delivered head explains 0.0° of it. Measuring the bar first was the point: had it read 2°, no head gate worth writing would have been possible here. Corroborated weakly by an independent detector and estimator — Apple Vision ear yaw, |r| = 0.34–0.40 against shuffled controls of 0.20–0.22. Enough to say a signal exists; not enough to call the magnitude measured.

9 excluded, 1 retracted The head is solved as far as this fixture allows

A rigid head fitted across all four views and all 150 frames, never triangulating a head landmark alone, now a pipeline stage. Performer 0 clears all four bands; performer 1 misses one — the p95 of per-frame agreement — by 1.18°. The band has not moved at any point.

The decomposition is the finding. Scored against a perfect head in the same frame, which cancels the reference-frame mismatch, our estimator is identical on both performers: 9.91° and 9.80° at p95. The floors differ by 2.16°; our own error differs by 0.11°. Performer 1 does not fail because our head is worse there. It fails because the frame mismatch is larger there — and the reference's own retained diagnostics show it is 20% less certain about that performer, on every camera. Which side of that disagreement is wrong is not decidable without ground truth, which is what the marker session is for.

The solve now reaches the delivered file. subject-*.body-track.npz carries a Head local rotation of median 25.81° / 21.72°, solved on 100% of frames, and the gate is verified to score those rotations. Only the head channel changed — every other joint agrees with the previous build to 1.6e-16 and root translation to exactly zero. It had not shipped because a detection cache keyed on line count served a file whose schema predated the head landmarks: the build delivered the constant and exited 0. That path now raises.

One exclusion has been retracted, and it was found by checking the rule rather than the head. The temporal weight is picked by argmin in-sample reprojection — but adding a penalty cannot improve a data fit, so that curve must rise monotonically and argmin must always land at zero. It does not. At a tolerance tight enough that the optimiser has demonstrably converged (results identical to four decimals across two settings), the curve is still non-monotonic — so it is not an iteration budget, it is a local minimum. A converged rule would select weight zero, which the anatomical travel gate rejects at 127°/frame. The rule reaches a defensible answer for an indefensible reason. Nothing was changed: every candidate repair separates solutions by thousandths of a pixel, and choosing among them by reprojection applies the very criterion shown to be unsound. A principled criterion for this parameter does not exist in this lane yet.

The last untried lever was pre-registered, and it lost. A physical criterion — pick the least smoothing whose head motion is physiologically possible, bounded at 800°/s from a figure already in the source — selects cleanly and identically on both performers: unregularised heads move at 945 and 2811°/s, comfortably impossible, and the selected weight lands at 300 and 299°/s, squarely human. And it scores worse — 22.19° and 23.37°, failing both performers against the incumbent’s 17.77 and 21.18. Published because it was written down first.

That result is worth more than a pass would have been. The head needs more smoothing than physiology can justify: at the selected weight it still moves 300°/s, and that motion is detector noise wearing a plausible speed. Every criterion computable without a reference has now failed for a stated reason — held-out cross-validation (noise correlated across cameras), the L-curve (replaced), argmin reprojection (unsound in principle), neck physiology (necessary, not sufficient). What remains would consult a reference, and using the gate’s own reference for it is gate-tuning. The shipped configuration is unchanged and its justification is written down as weak rather than dressed up.

Blind to — accuracy, always: this is parity against an instrument on a tracking metric. Eight mechanisms remain excluded with measurements — convergence, weight-grid resolution, thorax landmark choice, robust-loss specification, optimiser seed, the floor as a whole explanation, jaw rigidity, the smoothing chart, and a multi-point rigid torso fit (much worse: the torso is not rigid, the shoulders swing). The two levers that remain are already the lane's top two items: a model-constrained torso, and ground truth. Neither is head work. And the delivered artifacts still carry the old constant — the rebuild is outstanding.

ball yes, tip no The toes split: the ball of the foot survives its first test

The delivered Toes channel is a constant — 0.000° on every frame of both performers, exactly as the head was. Foot is the more dangerous case: it moves (18–32° median), so a naive “does it move?” gate passes it — yet the 19 landmark targets end at the ankle, so nothing observed constrains rotation about it.

Scored against the triangulated Foot→ToeBase direction, with a fixed axis as the control: on performer 0 the delivered foot beats the constant (13.9° vs 23.4°); on performer 1 a single unmoving axis would beat what we ship (43.4° vs 24.2°).

The two toe joints do not survive together. ToeBase, the ball, holds length to 5.0–9.6% — 2.0–2.4× the leg controls but better than the arm controls, nothing like HeadEnd’s 66–115%. ToeEnd, the tip, fails at 14–145%, differently on each foot of each performer — the signature of a point the detector invents. The ankle angle agrees: performer 0 holds 68–71° with 7° of spread, performer 1 has three times that and a third of frames outside anatomical range.

Blind to — accuracy entirely, and here it bites: both sides of the foot-axis comparison are ours, so it can show the delivered channel is uninformed but never which estimate is wrong when they disagree. Also blind to ground contact. Three things gate any build: MAMMA’s own feet as the bar (unmeasured), epipolar consistency of the toe 2D, and whether a ToeBase solve beats both controls — the constant and today’s delivered foot. A gate showing only “better than frozen” would pass a solve worse than what ships. Not started, deliberately.

26.5° / 20.7°Head motion a rebuild would deliver

Median local Head rotation per performer that the pipeline now produces. The artifact on disk has not been regenerated, so it still carries the identity quaternion on every frame; the rebuild is the outstanding step. Wiring it exposed two defects the gate is structurally blind to: a rigid fit is correct only up to a constant, and a pixel criterion has no notion of what a neck can do. Both fixed and tested.

05

Closed today — questions that will not come back

Defect — shipping path Every delivered character faces 180° the wrong way. The joints are driven correctly; the rig's left/right naming is mirrored relative to the mesh it drives, and the retarget turns that into a pure yaw. It survived because a self-consistent mirrored human still looks like a human — nothing in the pipeline had ever held a render against the footage it came from. That comparison now exists and should run on every capture.
Trap — every MAMMA comparison MAMMA's subject indices are not ours. Its body_id-00 is our subject 1. Both outputs are two-element lists indexed 0 and 1, nothing declares the order, and the swap is invisible in every per-subject statistic taken separately — it corrupts only the pairings, which is exactly what a parity table is made of. It was caught because a corroboration returned no signal (|r| = 0.21 / 0.03 against controls of 0.18 / 0.22); corrected, the same data gives 0.34 / 0.40. Correspondence is now derived from 3D pelvis agreement — matched pairs 41–55 mm, crossed pairs 1.36–1.38 m, a 25× margin — and asserted rather than assumed.
Retracted — my own defect A published conclusion was an artifact of a bug in the gate's gauge. The gate estimated its mean-removal over frames it was not scoring, inflating every filtered arm about 2×. That produced a tidy story — "filtering makes even the oracle worse, so it must be removing frames where the reference frames agree" — which was written up at length and never recomputed. Corrected, the filter helps: subject 0's agreement improves from 7.54° to 5.73°, and both degenerate controls still fail, so it cannot launder a bad head. The headline pass was never affected — the unfiltered arms' masks are all-true, so the defect was a no-op there. Lesson: an oracle behaving strangely means audit the instrument, not theorise. It is the one arm whose value you can predict.
Opened — the largest lever found so far Most of what we call "coherent joint-definition bias" is not per-joint at all. It is one 2D translation per camera, static rather than per-frame — and removing those four constants moves 3D error from 45.1 to 37.9 mm median, and 126.9 to 94.6 at p95. A shuffled control makes p95 worse, so the gain is real. The cause is not yet established: calibration error, a crop convention, or the shared part of a genuine convention offset. If it is four numbers rather than nineteen conventions, it is far cheaper to kill than the detector campaign assumes.
Closed A perfect veto of every bad detector observation is worth +0.05 mm. The triangulator's inlier gate already discards 98% of them. No veto of any provenance is worth building on this rig — SAM 3D closes entirely.
Closed The detector's tail is not the driver. It is the most visible difference in 2D and the least consequential in 3D. What remains is the bulk and the coherent bias — which is what the pseudo-label campaign attacks.
Corrected σ and visibility were never two channels being compared — they were the same information on opposite sides of one threshold. A confidence acts as a gate, not a weight.
Corrected A synthetic result is only as good as its noise model's agreement with the real error's shape. Ours matched the deciles and still put the mass in the wrong place.
Opened The solver measures each performer's own bone lengths, one set per subject per take — and the retarget throws them away. Restoring them recovers 19–35% of the body-proportion gap.
Corrected That finding was first written up at "44%" off a pooled median. The landmark population is bimodal — legs ~25 mm, arms ~180 mm — so the same statistic reads 2% on the other performer. A rank statistic over a bimodal population is not a summary.
Corrected "Rescaling the torso is harmful" was a bug in the measuring script, not a finding — an 80 mm origin offset. Corrected, torso scaling is the single largest proportion win on one performer.
Corrected The target on this page was wrong until today. 13.5 mm is MPJPE against a fitted reference, not marker error. On held-out markers the reference system scores 22.48 mm against a marker pipeline's own 21.62 — parity is a 0.86 mm gap between two ~21 mm instruments.
Closed Camera count. The geometry bound improves as one-over-root-N — six cameras buy 18% — but the real system does not move: the reference's own ablation is flat from four to eight cameras, because the detector dominates, not the geometry. Do not buy cameras for single-person accuracy. Buy them, if at all, for two-person, hands, or training-label purity.
Opened Occlusion is strongly correlated across views. Assuming independence predicts 6.2% of joints starved of views; the measured figure is 35.3% — a 5.7× miss on the one case we can check.
06

What happens next

Yours

Book the marker session

Rank 0 for the second time, and nothing else can unblock it. Weeks of lead time on the marker reference, days-to-weeks on genlock, plus performer releases covering ML training. Stronger option: build an owned multicam fixture and physical-constraint kit first, so it becomes bring markers to our stage rather than renting someone else's.

Yours

Two licence gates, both unmet, both blocking the detector work

NVIDIA's model licence is not archived against any model revision, so whether we may fine-tune SOMA-77 into commercial weights cannot be answered from what we hold — and the question of what its DINOv3 initialisation carries is still unsent. Separately and more urgently: performer releases covering ML training use. Training on owned footage is illegal without them, and no amount of engineering routes around it.

Study

Pose2Sim — the commercially clean cousin, doing what we do not

Same architecture as ours, but it scales a skeleton to each subject and runs IK against it. BSD-3, Apache-2.0 detector and IK, no restricted body model anywhere. Two things it may hand us at once: the pattern for the per-performer skeleton, and a fix for our systematic joint-convention bias — it reports the same failure mode and absorbs it in that same layer.

Do not inherit its sync — verified in source, it takes the correlation peak at integer frames only, which quantises to ±16.7 ms at 30 fps, or 83 mm at a fast wrist. OpenCap has the same defect and publishes no sync accuracy at all.

Next

Pseudo-label campaign for the detector

Train on our own triangulated labels from owned footage. It kills the ~18.6 px coherent bias by construction — there is no convention to cross — and attacks the bulk. Now the top engineering priority, because it is aimed at the half of the gap that still matters.

Study

Rig joint placement — attempted, band failed, reverted

The collarbone fix was tried and made things worse: the canonical round-trip control went from 0.00 to 46–67 mm on the legs, and the change was reverted the same hour. The reason is the useful part. No rig joint origin sits where its captured landmark does — the rig puts the hips at the pelvis while the leg joints hang 80 mm below and 90 mm to each side, and its hip separation is 180 mm against a measured 207. So the collarbone was a symptom. The real fix is the root and hip placement convention, and it belongs with the per-performer skeleton work rather than ahead of it.

Small

Land the weighting fix and its harness together

A committed script passes a parameter the committed source does not accept, so a clean checkout raises TypeError. Land them as one change, and do not flip the default — the fix is correct but no instrument for it exists yet.

Every figure carries the instrument that produced it and what that instrument is blind to. Numbers measured against MAMMA are structurally floored at ~10 mm; numbers from the synthetic fixture contain none of calibration, distortion, sync, soft-tissue or joint-definition error. Nothing here is an accuracy claim.
