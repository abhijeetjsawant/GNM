01

The honest position

No number in this project has ever been checked against ground truth.

Every millimetre figure is measured against MAMMA's own output — an estimate, not truth — or against synthetic data that contains none of calibration error, lens distortion, sync error, soft-tissue artefact or joint-definition error. All five are first-order on real footage.

The marker session is the only path to an accuracy claim, and it is not booked. Until it lands, every band below is a consistency band, not an accuracy band.

How this board is measured against MAMMA, from 2026-09-02: the Substitution Ladder. MAMMA divided as its own code divides it, ours beside each stage, one instrument per rung with its blindness, figures read only from on-disk reports, and a history so a change that helps one rung and hurts another is caught. Replace one part at a time; end-to-end is the last rung. From 2026-09-02 every rung on the ladder page, and every part on the progress page, carries a bar chart of ours beside MAMMA's, labelled in plain words whether lower or higher is better, generated from the same reports so the two pages cannot disagree. This board stays the written record; the pictures live there. Plan of record: docs/SUBSTITUTION_LADDER.md; execution order and gates, reviewed independently by Codex and Sol: docs/LADDER_EXECUTION_PLAN.md. Two things that review found and the source confirms: THORAX_SMOOTHING_FRAMES in the delivered head solve was selected on the MAMMA oracle arm, a shipped constant fitted on a research artifact, open until re-selected; and the delivered foot has been solved from ToeBase since f6a4973, so the foot-axis figure above now scores the solver against its own input and is retired as a gate.

The provenance audit ran on 2026-09-02 (ladder step I8). Eighty-nine constants, weights and calibration inputs across the fifty-five functions the delivery build reaches: one leak, the thorax smoothing window above; two declared items, the camera rig that is MAMMA's calibration and the licence-scoped example footage, both retired by the hardware lane; 17 constants of unknown origin, none MAMMA-derived; 69 clean. No leak into weights is possible because nothing on the delivery path is trained here. The audit script exits non-zero while a leak stands, so it can gate a build. Re-selected on synthetic truth with our own detector's noise, the window has an interior optimum at 9 frames (bracket 5–9) at every playback speed that reaches real thorax motion; the fixture under-reaches real speed and the injected noise is white, both of which push the answer wider, so 9 is an upper bound and 15 is unsupported. At 15 the frame lags 1.8 frames and loses 32% of peak yaw rate. Changed to 9 on 2026-09-02 on the user's decision; the audit exits clean. The head gate re-run with the MAMMA arm reporting only: gated medians improved on both performers, 6.39→5.52° and 8.25→7.19°; p95 went 16.4→20.0° on performer 0 (worse) and 27.5→22.2° on performer 1 (better); and the gate's own floor, MAMMA's head through our frames, rose from 3.6 / 3.9° to 4.3 / 4.3°, as a narrower window must, because it leaves more jitter in the reference frame and that jitter is charged to every arm. Performer 0's verdict flipped from pass to fail on the p95 band. Reverting on that would be selecting the constant on the MAMMA arm, which is the leak all over again, so 9 stands and the verdict is recorded as it is. What the flip says is about the gate's frame as much as the head: a band the reference frame's own jitter can move is measuring the frame. The paired block bootstrap makes that concrete: head-minus-oracle p95 in the same frame, on identical draws, is 1.8° on performer 0 and 5.3° on performer 1, and the candidate's p95 rise on performer 0 sits inside the floor's own rise. But the oracle now passes the 20° band on only 84–88% of draws, where at window 15 it passed on all of them: the fixed band is near miscalibrated for this frame. That is recorded and not acted on; moving the band on this evidence would select on the MAMMA arm. The gap to the floor is now a standing figure on the ladder. The delivered body-track.npz files still carry the window-15 head until the one rebuild after the facing fix lands; the ladder's head figures reflect the code, not the shipped file.

The target is not 13.5 mm, and this board said so until 2026-08-30. That figure is MPJPE against a fitted reference — Vicon markers passed through a model fit — and it is read off a chart, not a published table. On held-out markers, the measurement behind the parity claim, the reference system scores 22.48 mm against a marker pipeline's own 21.62 mm. State three numbers, never one: MPJPE 12.96 · PVE 17.18 · held-out marker 22.48.

And this is why the session gate matters more than the number. Two marker-grade instruments sit 0.86 mm apart at ~22 mm, so marker ground truth cannot discriminate below about 20 mm. The session can tell us whether we are at 25 or 45. It can never tell us whether we are at 13 or 18 — a sub-20 mm claim needs a different instrument entirely, a body scan or synthetic truth.

Measured · known problem

2D detection — SOMA-77 / Apple Vision / MediaPipe

Re-measured with controls on 2026-09-02 (ladder step I3), and two earlier figures are retired. The "2.2× vs MAMMA" compared our 2D against raw triangulation with MAMMA's 2D against a fit that had consumed those very landmarks: two denominators, so it said nothing. And the "~18.6 px coherent bias" is about three times too large: the measured per-camera common mode is 3.8 px at 1280, about 11 px native. Three separate reports now stand, on three references that never share an axis. Reference-free cross-view self-agreement: 2.77 px one-sided at 1280, p95 9.4. Against MAMMA's projected joints, in millimetres after re-triangulation: 43.3 with no offsets, 37.8 after four per-camera offsets fitted on the other half of the frames, 35.7 with a free per-frame translation, which is the ceiling any coordinate repair can reach. In pixels against the same joints: 6.1 px at 1280, labelled as disagreement with a fitter, not error.

The decision rule was written before the first figure, and it sends the lane to the pseudo-label campaign. 82.5% of the detector's 3D cost survives the best possible per-view shift; that residue is per-joint error no offset fix reaches. The offset fix fails its own rule on one clause: shuffling the four offsets across cameras keeps 90% of the gain, because all four point the same way, up and left by a similar amount. It is not four numbers; it is one shared crop or resize convention in the detector's preprocessing, and a single global vector, held out, beats the four. Whether it is a detector convention or a calibration error the instrument cannot say, because the rig is MAMMA's. Two controls pass by design: our own 3D reprojected passes the self-agreement band, which is why that report is a diagnostic and not a gate, and putting the skull-joint "nose" back into the population moves nothing, so that exclusion is conservative rather than load-bearing. Lag-1 autocorrelation measured at 0.63–0.84, not the head lane's 0.99. One registered follow-up rule is left failing on its rotated-offset clause and restated for the next run rather than rewritten after the numbers.

And it discards 60 of 77 joints — but it does not delete them. The adapter maps 17 of SOMA-77's 77, dropping every finger chain, both toe joints, HeadEnd, Jaw and the clavicles. All 77 are nevertheless retained on disk, every person, every frame, all four cameras — proven by 20,043 identity checks with zero mismatches. This board previously said measuring them "needs a worker re-run, not a re-read". That was wrong. Toes, HeadEnd, Jaw and 30 finger joints are measurable now. nose is index 6, the Head skeletal joint, and sits behind the eyes.

The first of the dropped joints has now been measured, and it failed. HeadEnd — the head lead the plan called most promising — triangulates a rigid skull segment at 194–254 mm against an anatomical 110–140, with a standard deviation exceeding the anatomical quantity itself. That is the phalanx pattern one length scale up. Head landmarks are SOMA-77's weakest region, and the separation is in the tail: cross-view pair spread p95 reaches 1,805 mm on the eyes against 76–141 mm on every limb control. Scored on one frame set against the other two integrated adapters, Apple Vision's ear axis is the only head axis that stays inside its own detector's body-control range — and on segments of nearly equal length it is 5.5–6.4× tighter than SOMA-77's skull axis. It survives a null template that predicts the ears from nose, eyes and neck with no ear evidence: the null is 7–20× worse. This is regional, not a detector swap — SOMA-77's body is better, and Apple Vision's ears resolve on only two thirds of frames at 2.4–2.7 cameras.

But the head's 2D are the best thing this detector produces. Symmetric epipolar distance per camera pair — the one test that is blind to depth by construction — puts every head landmark on both detectors at 0.29–0.78× its own detector's body control, never above 1.0. Four cameras agree on a single 3D head point better than they agree on a knee. So the head's failure is not detection; it is entirely depth — rays to a 120–160 mm feature at 5 m are near-parallel relative to it, so excellent 2D still triangulates into decimetres of scatter along the viewing direction. That is an estimator problem, and it is the same finding that sent the fingers to a model-constrained fit — which passed this test at 1.2×, less cleanly than the head does.

The ear lead was measured twice more and is now closed. Scored on direction rather than length, the ear axis scatters at 21–41° of yaw, larger than the whole head-on-torso signal — because a differential depth error is first order in an axis's direction and second order in its length, so a length invariant cannot score direction. Put into a rigid-head fit's objective instead, the ears made it worse, carrying 1.3–1.6× the reprojection residual of SOMA-77's head joints despite being the most cross-view-consistent landmarks measured. An ear is a surface landmark that slides over the skull as the head turns — every camera sees the same apparent point, and it is not a fixed place on the bone. That is this lane's founding detector argument arriving at the head, and it says the head needs landmarks that stay on the bone, not a wider baseline.

And then the ear axis lost its recommendation to a second measurement, which is the more useful half. All of the above scores a segment's length. Scored on direction against MAMMA, the same ear axis scatters at 21–41° of yaw — larger than the entire head-on-torso signal it would explain. The mechanism generalises: for an axis roughly perpendicular to the camera's depth direction, a differential depth error between its endpoints is first order in direction and second order in length, so a length invariant cannot score direction — the companion to this lane's standing "reprojection cannot score depth". The ears belong in the objective of a multi-frame fit, not on a direct path to a rotation channel.

Blind to — no ground truth. Scored against MAMMA's own fit, which is deflated because its fit consumed the landmarks it is scored against. The head comparison is stability, which is convention-free; it says nothing about which landmark is anatomically closer to truth, and an ear, an eye and a joint centre are different points under different definitions.

Fill & temporal smoothing

Touches every joint and moves already-triangulated ones by 5.6–7.9 mm median, with a tail past 0.9 m. Those large moves are outlier repair, not damage — joints it moves furthest had 34–46× the raw temporal acceleration. It is the pipeline's de facto outlier filter.

Measured on a perfect detector for the first time (ladder step I2, 2026-09-02). MAMMA's own skeleton projected into the four cameras and run through the real pipeline comes back 1.15 mm median from what was projected, 4.2 mm p95. Raw triangulation on that input is exact to 1e-8 mm, so association and triangulation cost nothing on perfect 2D and the whole floor is this stage, largest where motion is fastest: wrists 1.6, ankles 1.3–1.5, root 0.8. Against the same reference our real capture sits at 36–41 mm, so the reconstruction is about 3% of that gap and everything else is upstream of 2D. With MAMMA-grade 2D noise injected the floor is 4.6 mm; a one-frame shift on one camera costs 6.7 mm, the same order, which is why sync is not a second-order problem. A frozen skeleton reconstructs with exactly zero reprojection error and lands 695 mm off, the cleanest demonstration yet that no reprojection figure is an accuracy figure.

And the stage's actual job is now measured (ladder step I7, 2026-09-02), on synthetic truth with views dropped. When a joint is lost for a run of frames, the shape real occlusion has, the sequence solve lands 7.7 mm from truth against 51.5 mm for a line drawn through the gap, on every bootstrap draw. When the loss is a single isolated frame the line wins, 1.6 against 2.1 mm, and that is not a defect: two good neighbours one frame apart beat a solve. The smoother is zero-phase, it delays nothing even at three times its window, so a lag band on it discriminates nothing; what it is answerable to is attenuation, 1% of peak speed lost at the shipped window and 17% at three times it, and a detector's worst-1% spike that goes 21 → 79 mm without it. A sync candidate fell out: reprojecting the smoothed 3D into a held-out camera, three cameras read zero lag and D001 reads +0.9 frames; shifting D001 by one frame collapses all four. One measurement, for the hardware lane, never corrected on this rig. And half of the thorax-window puzzle is the detector's heavy tail: a heavy-tailed noise arm reproduces 2.5° of the real 9-versus-15 gap where white noise reproduces none; the rest is outside every synthetic arm.

Blind to — whether any move went toward truth. MAMMA's fitted mesh is itself 2.6–2.9× smoothed, and its triangulation is correlated with the 2D being smoothed. Both candidate references are banned for scoring this. The oracle above is blind to everything upstream of 2D, including joint-definition error, and runs on a take with no dropped views, so the sequence solve's actual job is never exercised.

Calibration, sync, lens distortion

Every result runs on MAMMA's camera rig, which is MPI-derived and barred from shipping. We have never measured a sync residual on anything of ours. Half a frame at 30 Hz costs 29–48 mm at our joint speeds — and MAMMA's own genlocked rig still slipped two frames.

A sixth absent term, and it cuts both ways. Rolling shutter is tracked nowhere — per-row readout means genlocked cameras still sample head and feet at different instants, and measured readouts run 8.7–26.8 ms against a 5 ms budget. But that same row structure is the cheapest sub-millisecond sync instrument there is: a flash lights only a band of scanlines, and the band's row index encodes sub-frame phase to 0.3–0.5 ms under an MIT licence. Global shutter removes the error term and the instrument.

The dominant answer is neither. A GPIO hardware trigger holds under 100 ns — three to four orders of magnitude better than any software method. If the cameras accept one, nothing else here matters.

Consequence — no shipped constant may be fitted on a MAMMA-derived artifact. An owned multicam fixture can precede the marker session, would retire this, and is now load-bearing for three separate questions: sync, the sequence solve, and Step 4's training data.

One exclusion has been retracted, and it was found by checking the rule rather than the head. The temporal weight is picked by argmin in-sample reprojection — but adding a penalty cannot improve a data fit, so that curve must rise monotonically and argmin must always land at zero. It does not. At a tolerance tight enough that the optimiser has demonstrably converged (results identical to four decimals across two settings), the curve is still non-monotonic — so it is not an iteration budget, it is a local minimum. A converged rule would select weight zero, which the anatomical travel gate rejects at 127°/frame. The rule reaches a defensible answer for an indefensible reason. Nothing was changed: every candidate repair separates solutions by thousandths of a pixel, and choosing among them by reprojection applies the very criterion shown to be unsound. A principled criterion for this parameter does not exist in this lane yet.

The last untried lever was pre-registered, and it lost. A physical criterion — pick the least smoothing whose head motion is physiologically possible, bounded at 800°/s from a figure already in the source — selects cleanly and identically on both performers: unregularised heads move at 945 and 2811°/s, comfortably impossible, and the selected weight lands at 300 and 299°/s, squarely human. And it scores worse — 22.19° and 23.37°, failing both performers against the incumbent’s 17.77 and 21.18. Published because it was written down first.

That result is worth more than a pass would have been. The head needs more smoothing than physiology can justify: at the selected weight it still moves 300°/s, and that motion is detector noise wearing a plausible speed. Every criterion computable without a reference has now failed for a stated reason — held-out cross-validation (noise correlated across cameras), the L-curve (replaced), argmin reprojection (unsound in principle), neck physiology (necessary, not sufficient). What remains would consult a reference, and using the gate’s own reference for it is gate-tuning. The shipped configuration is unchanged and its justification is written down as weak rather than dressed up.

The miss is real in direction and unresolvable in magnitude. A moving-block bootstrap — 2000 draws, candidate and oracle on identical draws — puts performer 0 at P(pass) = 1.00 at every block length, the first bootstrapped pass here rather than a point estimate. Performer 1 fails on about three draws in four, so the direction holds and the verdict is unchanged — but its interval spans the band, so “misses by 1.18°” is a number this take cannot resolve. The oracle passes at P = 1.00 on both, which attributes the fragility to us and not to the gate.

So the binding constraint has stopped being the estimator and become the fixture. A head improvement of 1° could not be demonstrated here even if it were real — it would land inside an interval eleven degrees wide. Twelve mechanisms have been excluded; a thirteenth would not be measurable. The robust quantity is the paired gap against a perfect head, 3.95–4.25°, stable across block lengths — that is what a fixture with ground truth would turn into a target. A wide interval is not permission to call a failure a pass, and the bands have never moved.

The head landmark error is MODEL error, not resolution error — measured, and it withdrew our own hypothesis. The head is 30.6 px across at the width the pipeline runs, which looked like the whole story. Running the detector at native 3840 grew it to 91.4 px, exactly 3×, and the within-head 3D scatter moved 1.05× and 0.89× — against a pre-registered expectation of ~3×. Three times the pixels bought 5%, and slightly worse on the failing performer.

The pair of numbers that disagree is the finding. Head-fit reprojection improved ~10% while 3D scatter did not improve at all. A real noise reduction moves both. Improving agreement between cameras while leaving the 3D position as uncertain is what happens when every camera is confidently wrong the same way — the views agree, the rays still miss the skull, and sharper pixels only make the agreement crisper. Consistency is not correctness, which is the ears finding generalised to the whole head set.

Same architecture as ours, but it scales a skeleton to each subject and runs IK against it. BSD-3, Apache-2.0 detector and IK, no restricted body model anywhere. Two things it may hand us at once: the pattern for the per-performer skeleton, and a fix for our systematic joint-convention bias — it reports the same failure mode and absorbs it in that same layer.

Do not inherit its sync — verified in source, it takes the correlation peak at integer frames only, which quantises to ±16.7 ms at 30 fps, or 83 mm at a fast wrist. OpenCap has the same defect and publishes no sync accuracy at all.

Rig joint placement — attempted, band failed, reverted

The collarbone fix was tried and made things worse: the canonical round-trip control went from 0.00 to 46–67 mm on the legs, and the change was reverted the same hour. The reason is the useful part. No rig joint origin sits where its captured landmark does — the rig puts the hips at the pelvis while the leg joints hang 80 mm below and 90 mm to each side, and its hip separation is 180 mm against a measured 207. So the collarbone was a symptom. The real fix is the root and hip placement convention, and it belongs with the per-performer skeleton work rather than ahead of it. D2 (2026-09-02) confirmed this from the other side: the clavicle half of that fix, aimed from the rig's own shoulder origin, is right and is built on branch ladder/D2, and its oracle reads 67 / 79 mm for exactly this reason: it is the first direction measured in the rig's frame, so it is the first that can see the 80 mm root offset. With that offset taken out in the instrument it reads 0.5 / 0.1 mm. And a second constraint surfaced: the rig's pivot is 60–170 mm from the shoulder landmark, so the clavicle chain jitters wherever the landmarks wander. Root placement and the per-performer clavicle are what discriminate; the decision on how to ship D2 is the user's. D3 (2026-09-03) shipped the per-performer skeleton and closed the export gap it exposed; the shoulder construction is D5's.
