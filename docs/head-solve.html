Head Orientation Solve

The head we shipped was a constant, welded to the chest. This is the solve that replaced it, what it was scored against, and the four qualifications that belong in the same breath as the word “passes”.

Fixture 4 cameras · 2 performers · 150 frames · ~5 m Status passes its gate · on the delivery path Full working docs/HEAD_ORIENTATION_MEASURED.md

Where it stands

A rigid head is fitted across all four views and all 150 frames at once — no head landmark is ever triangulated on its own. It clears every band of a pre-registered gate on both performers, at one temporal weight chosen by a rule that never consults the reference. Two degenerate controls fail, and fail differently. An oracle arm confirms the bands are reachable.

And it now ships. The solve is a pipeline stage, and a delivered track carries 26.5° and 20.7° of median head rotation where it carried the identity quaternion on every frame. Wiring it exposed two defects the gate is structurally blind to — see the corrections below.

Qualification 1Two of the four bands are knob settings for the candidate — the fit regularises exactly what they measure. They discriminate the controls. The substance is per-frame agreement alone.
Qualification 2Performer 0’s margin on that band is a coin flip — a moving-block bootstrap puts P(fail) at 0.48, against the oracle’s 0.02.
Qualification 3It is parity, not accuracy. The reference is an instrument, never truth. If its head is smoothly wrong, this fit is wrong with it.
Qualification 4The pass is not uniform. On three of four half-takes the fit sits within 1.7° of a perfect head; on performer 0’s first half it sits 8° away.

Why the shipped head is the sharpest control. Its spread is exactly 0.00° — it cannot move relative to the chest, because one line of the retarget assigns it the torso’s frame. Yet on frame-to-frame world rotation, the metric anyone reaches for first, it scores 6.64° against the reference’s 6.62°. A head carrying no information at all sits within 0.3% of parity on the obvious measurement. That is why the gate scores tracking and ships a control that fails it.

What these pictures are, and what they are not

The three frames are evenly spaced and were chosen before any angle was computed — an earlier version of this figure greedy-picked the frames that maximised the very angle it displayed, and did not say so. All four cameras are shown for the same instant rather than one, because in a single view the difference often lies along that camera’s viewing ray: the median separation between the two gizmos is only 3–5 px at source scale. Where the difference looks small, projection is hiding it.

A projected overlay is evidence of placement and never of accuracy — error along a camera’s own ray is invisible in it. Nothing here validates the solve; the gate above does that, in three dimensions. The gizmo is drawn at the fitted Head landmark inside the skull, not at the solve’s template origin, which sits near the collar and which an earlier version of this figure used without saying so.

5ideas tried and rejected

Including two of my own recommendations. Listed below.

What got it over the line — three of the four are anatomy, none is a tuned parameter

Load-bearingFit, don’t triangulate.

The head’s 2D turned out to be more cross-view consistent than the body’s — 0.29–0.78× the body control — so the failure was depth, not detection, and an estimator could reach it. Per-frame disagreement p95 fell from 65–89° to about 20°.

Load-bearingSmooth the neck, not the head in world.

The head in world carries the torso’s motion too, so a world-space prior fights the body while under-constraining the joint that is actually smooth. Jitter fell by an order of magnitude.

ForcedRepair the gate’s own reference frame.

It was built from raw triangulation and scored against a fitted one. Confirmed independently: through the old frame, the oracle itself fails — so the repair was forced, not convenient.

DecorationAnchor the skull to the neck.

Anatomically right, and ablating it still passes. Retained because it costs nothing, not because it earned anything.

Rejected, with the reason

RejectedHeadEnd as a skull axis — the lead the plan called most promising.

A rigid bone measures 194–254 mm against an anatomical 110–140, with a standard deviation exceeding the anatomical quantity itself.

RejectedA second detector’s ears, twice — both times my own recommendation.

First as a direct driver: their per-frame yaw scatters 21–41°, larger than the whole signal. Then inside the fit: they carry the worst residual despite being the most cross-view consistent landmarks measured, because an ear is a surface landmark that slides over the skull as the head turns.

RejectedA support-conditioned prior, and per-landmark inverse-variance weighting.

Correct in principle, worth 0.1–0.7° and 0.005 px respectively — both inside the noise. Rejected on their own criteria, without consulting the gate.

What I got wrong

All of it was caught by an instrument or a reviewer rather than by inspection, and all of it is corrected in the record.

RetractedA published conclusion was an artifact of my own defect.

The gate estimated its mean-removal over frames it was not scoring, inflating every filtered arm about 2×. That produced a tidy story — “filtering makes even the oracle worse” — which I wrote up at length and never recomputed. Corrected, filtering helps: performer 0’s agreement improves from 7.54° to 5.73°. The headline was never affected. An oracle behaving strangely means audit the instrument, not theorise.

FalseA commit message of mine stated a causal claim that is not true.

I said anchoring the head created the selection curve’s interior minimum. It predates the anchor by one commit — where the same rule defect was already visible in a printed sweep and I said nothing until it cost a pass.

DeletedThe commit that declared the pass removed the evidence an earlier commit promised was auditable.

186 lines, including the full weight sweep and two whole sections. Restored.

CrossedThe reference’s subject indices are not ours.

Its body 00 is our performer 1. Invisible in every per-subject statistic taken separately; it corrupts only the pairings, which is exactly what a parity table is made of. Now derived from 3D agreement with a 25× margin and asserted, never assumed.

Every figure here regenerates from tools/head/ against retained inputs. The gate measures parity with an instrument on a tracking metric — not accuracy, which nothing in this lane can yet claim. The solve is now a pipeline stage; the neck still keeps the torso frame, which is deliberate and unmeasured rather than solved.
